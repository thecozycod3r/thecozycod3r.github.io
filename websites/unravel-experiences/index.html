Unravel Experiences

A travel company that sells short trips built around one remarkable stay. With the Unravel team I rebuilt the site so each trip, called an Original, reads like an itinerary with a price, and added a members’ area for Unravel Access.

WebsiteRevamp, member login and motion, with the Unravel team
Visit unravelexperiences.com

The brief

What the site had to do.

Unravel sells experiences such as three nights in a Jaipur jeweller’s haveli or a weekend at a Pune vineyard, priced per couple. The old site described places; it had to show what the days actually look like, what is included and what it costs, give frequent travellers a reason to sign up, and keep a separate offer for companies booking staff travel.

Design language

How it looks, and why.

  • White pages, ink type and large, warm hotel photography in rounded cards; the photos do the selling.
  • One ember orange, used only where you act or where value is shown: the main buttons, the day markers of an itinerary and the “Included” labels.
  • A loader where the U rolls in like an unspooling thread and sets the wordmark down behind it, the brand name animated.
  • Unravel Access gets its own dark panel with a metallic member card, the one place the page goes dark.

Typography

  • Everything

    Plus Jakarta Sans

    One family from 400 to 800 for headlines, prices and body copy. Headlines italicise their second half (“Experiences across India”, “Live in the jeweller’s house”), which gives the voice a travel-writing lilt without a second typeface.

  • Labels

    Plus Jakarta Sans, spaced capitals

    Short eyebrows such as “UNRAVEL ORIGINALS · INDIA” and “THE JOURNEY” sit above each section, so a long page still scans in seconds.

Decisions

Why it works the way it does.

  • Every Original is an itinerary

    Each trip page lays the days out on a thread, from “Day one · Arrival” to “Until next time”, followed by what is included, the neighbourhood and an FAQ, so a visitor can picture the trip before asking about it.

  • Price up front, asked for once

    Cards show a “from” price per couple, and each trip has a sticky reservation card with the price, a “Get my exact price” request, a WhatsApp option and the cancellation terms.

  • A real login for members

    Unravel Access runs on Supabase Auth: sign in or create an account, a member dashboard and member trips, with an admin panel for the team.

  • No map where the address is private

    An area map was tried on trip pages and then removed. The neighbourhood is described in words, and the exact address is shared on confirmation.
