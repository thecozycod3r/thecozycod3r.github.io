The brief

What the site had to do.

The estate’s old domain was dead and its story lived in Instagram images of certificates. Most coffee sites are written for people buying a bag; Nandipura sells Arabica micro-lots and Robusta parchment to roasters and exporters. The site had to earn a buyer’s trust in one scroll.

Design language

How it looks, and why.

  • Dark, forest-floor palette taken from under the canopy: near-black ink, moss and a cream that reads like unbleached paper.
  • Two warm accents from the crop itself — cherry red for ripe coffee cherries, honey for the wild honey the estate also sells.
  • Editorial layout with very large headlines and long, generous line lengths, closer to a magazine feature than a shop.
  • Small uppercase fact strip (SINCE THE 1800s · 3,400 FT MSL · RAINFOREST ALLIANCE) so proof sits next to the promise.

Typography

  • Display

    Libre Caslon Display

    Caslon was the everyday type of colonial-era print in India when the estate was founded, in the late 1800s. At headline size it reads like an estate ledger or a planters’ notice: sharp, high-contrast and rarely seen on modern sites.

  • Italic & small heads

    Libre Caslon Text

    The Display cut has no italic and its hairlines thin out below about 24px, so quotes, press lines and small headings drop to the sturdier Text cut of the same family.

  • Text

    Libre Franklin

    A plain American gothic of the same era, for the facts a buyer scans: altitude, certification, process, lot type.

Decisions

Why it works the way it does.

  • “Enquire about lots”, not “Shop”

    They sell green coffee in bulk. A cart would have been the wrong promise, so every call to action is an enquiry that lands in the estate’s inbox.

  • Only verified facts

    Founding year, altitude, shade trees, Rainforest Alliance certification and the CoffeeReview blind cupping all come from the estate’s own documents. Nothing was written to sound impressive.

  • Reviews from real visits

    The estate’s Google reviews are about visiting, not drinking the coffee, so they are quoted on the Visit page with the reviewers’ names rather than used as product praise.

  • A journal for search

    Long-form posts on process and lots give roasters something to find on Google and give the estate a place to publish harvest updates.
