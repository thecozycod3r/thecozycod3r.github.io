Heritage members’ club, Yercaud, Tamil Nadu, 2026

YercaudClub

A hill club founded in 1897, with no website of its own. Rebuilt from a tariff card, a souvenir booklet and the club’s own photographs into a four-page site for members, affiliates and guests, now live on the club’s own domain.

  • Design, build and research

Cutive

The brief

What the site had to do.

The club had a printed tariff card, a dark red YC crest and a stack of WhatsApp photographs. Members of 18 affiliated clubs had no way to check visiting rates, and guests had no way to learn what the club actually offers. The site had to feel as old as the institution without feeling dusty.

Design language

How it looks, and why.

  • Near-black from header to footer, chosen by the club after seeing light, stone and dark versions side by side.
  • Yercaud red, sampled from the crest, appears only on things you can press: buttons, the selected tab, the member/guest switch.
  • The crest stands alone in the header as the home button, with no box or wordmark competing with it.
  • Photography runs edge to edge; every facility and chamber is shown with the club’s own pictures, not stock.

Typography

  • Display

    Cutive

    The crest’s own lettering is a typewriter slab serif. Cutive is the closest open face, so every headline reads in the same voice as the club’s logo and stationery.

  • Text

    Manrope

    A quiet geometric sans so rates, timings and tables stay legible at small sizes. The typewriter face is kept to headings, where it can carry character without tiring the eye.

Decisions

Why it works the way it does.

  • Every number comes from the club

    Rates, the 18 affiliations and the founding year are taken from the 2026 tariff card. Where public sources disagreed (an old phone number, a street name the club no longer uses), the club’s word won.

  • Designed with the club, round by round

    Three directions went to the club: the original cream, a stone-toned version in the crest’s typeface, and red on black. Their feedback — cleaner header, less red at the bottom, keep the typewriter headings — shaped the final site.

  • A member/guest switch instead of two tables

    Chamber tariffs differ for members and guests. One toggle swaps every price on the page, so nobody has to read across a dense grid to find their row.

  • Real pages, not a single-page app

    Each page is prerendered to static HTML at build time, so it loads instantly on hill-station mobile data and every section is indexable by search engines.

  • History, written from the souvenir

    The /history page was written from the club’s own souvenir booklet, so the story is theirs, not a template’s.
