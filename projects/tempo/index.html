An in-house task manager that replaced Asana for the whole agency: client boards, automatic routing to the right specialist, approval statuses that ping the right Slack channel, and reports on late work, all on a free database tier.

The problem

Asana worked. We just didn't own it.

I had already rolled Asana out company-wide (the Asana story), with rules that routed every task to the right person. The rules were tailor-made and the team trusted them. But pricing grew with every seat, and every change meant working around someone else's product.

My first redesign tried to improve the workflow. The team pushed back, so I studied the live Asana rules one by one and rebuilt them as they were. Improvements became opt-in switches, not defaults.

The solution

The same workflow, owned outright.

Roster-Driven Routing

Each client has a roster: account manager, Meta, Google, SEO, creative. A task assigned to a role lands on the right person automatically, and edits go back to the client's strategist.

Statuses That Talk

The agency's own 11 statuses. Moving a task to Out for Approval notifies the account manager and posts in the client's Slack channel through a Tempo bot.

Tidy Up

One page of late work and approvals stuck five working days or more, with one-click Done, New date or On hold, and Undo. Ages count working days and skip holidays.

Team Report

Completed work per person and team over time, visible only to founders and the creative lead. Completed tasks are logged before the 15-day auto-bin, so history survives.

Judgement calls

Rules in the database, not the browser.

  • Five access levels, from founder to team member, enforced by database triggers and row-level security. A member can comment and move status; only managers can reassign or edit.
  • Copy before you improve. Matching the old tool exactly made the switch a non-event for the team.
  • Free tier on purpose. About 55 MB of data a year against a 500 MB limit, with creatives kept as Drive links, and a documented path to a paid plan if it ever gets close.
  • Live Asana boards were imported like-for-like, so the switch-over did not lose open work.
