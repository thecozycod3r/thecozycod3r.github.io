One place where every client's ad spend, leads and sales outcomes meet. A cloud data pipeline across Meta, Google and client CRMs feeds a dashboard, AI insights, automated monthly reports and alerts. The problem

Spend in one tab, leads in another.

Account managers at DAM ran close to thirty client accounts. Spend lived in Meta Ads Manager and Google Ads. Leads lived in each client's own Google Sheet, every one with different columns, date formats and status words. Whether a lead was relevant, visited the site or booked lived in a third place again.

So the question every client actually asks — “what did my money buy?” — took an afternoon of copy-paste to answer, and the monthly report was rebuilt by hand every month.

Three sources per client, none joined

Cost per qualified lead was guesswork

The solution

A pipeline first, a dashboard second.

I built the data layer before the screens. A nightly cloud job pulls every account from the ad platforms and every client's lead sheet, cleans and joins them, and publishes one mart per client. Everything else — the dashboard, the insights, the reports, the alerts — reads from those marts, so no two views can disagree on a number.

Performance Dashboard

Spend, leads, cost per lead and funnel outcomes per client, with free date-range comparison and creative-level views. Staff and client logins see only what they are scoped to.

Insights That Do Arithmetic, Not Guessing

An Insights tab opens on up to four act / watch / working cards computed from plain thresholds — spend with zero results, cost 1.5× the average, an engine 20% cheaper. ‘Ask why’ then hands the card to AI with the right dataset.

One-Click Monthly Reports

Generates the client's monthly Word report. The pipeline computes every figure; AI only writes the words, and a check rejects any number the model invents.

Overnight Anomaly Alerts

Each campaign is compared with its own trailing 7 days — spend spikes, silent delivery, cost-per-lead jumps — and a Slack digest lands every morning with deep links into the ad account.

Competitor Ads

Pulls competitors' live ads from the Meta and Google ad libraries on demand and summarises the angles they are running, inside the client's own view.

Client Brief

Each client's strategy brief lives in the tool. AI drafts it from the website, call notes or an old deck; an account manager approves each section before anything downstream reads it.

One platform

From internal tool to product.

Clients kept asking for the view the account managers had, so Cerno became one platform with one login. Staff, client logins and businesses that sign up on their own all use the same site; what each person sees follows the email they have proven, not a separate deployment.

Access Follows Identity

Any sign-in method works, but agency-level access needs a confirmed address. Each client login is scoped to its own accounts, checked server-side on every request.

Self-Serve Onboarding

A business names itself, connects its own Google Ads and Meta accounts through OAuth, and gets the same dashboard the agency uses, filled by the same pipeline.

Week on Week

Any campaign, ad set or ad as eight whole Monday-to-Sunday weeks side by side. A client asked for it; it shipped to staff and client views together.

SEO Next to Paid

Search Console and GA4 sit beside the ad data, and a panel matches paid search terms to organic rankings to show where SEO would lower the cost of paid.

Judgement calls

The decisions that made it trustworthy.

  • Numbers come from code, words from AI. A model never computes a figure that reaches a client — reports are reconciled against the raw lead data before release (58 of 58 client-months matched exactly).
  • Alerts tuned on real data, not defaults. DAM campaigns average under one lead a day, so a naive "spent money, zero leads" rule fired on most healthy campaigns. Lead-volume gates and a 2× threshold cut noise to about ten alerts a day across fifteen clients.
  • A green job is not proof. An early cloud run reported success while silently publishing empty data. I added guards that refuse to publish when fewer than half the clients built, and a daily freshness watchdog that judges data age, not run status.
  • Knowing when not to ship. An ad-level verdict engine works for e-commerce clients but lead-gen data was too thin to judge individual ads — so I scoped it to sales accounts and documented what data would unlock the rest.
