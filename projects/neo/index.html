Neo

An SEO and GEO content platform that writes, publishes and tracks client blogs, and measures whether Google and AI assistants actually mention the client. Built to replace a paid subscription, running on the team's own Claude plans.

The problem

A monthly bill for a tool we could not change.

DAM sold SEO and GEO (getting cited by ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity) on a third-party content tool. It worked, but it was a monthly bill for a tool DAM could not change, and the team needed reports it could edit before they went to a client.

The brief I set myself: match it feature for feature, in DAM's own brand, so anyone with a Claude account can use it and everyone sees every client's work.

The solution

A shared hub, with the work done on the team's own Macs.

The website and database are shared; the heavy writing runs on small ‘runners’ installed on team members' Macs, each using that person's Claude plan. The site queues a job, the first free runner picks it up, and the result lands back in the hub for review. No company API key, no per-seat bill.

Articles, Brief to Draft

Keyword → outline (stops for human review) → article with internal and external links, brand-matched banner image, then a draft straight into the client's Webflow or Squarespace.

AI Visibility Tracking

Weekly checks of tracked prompts across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI: is the client named, which pages are cited, which competitors keep showing up, and the sentiment of each answer.

Rank Tracking + Search Console

Keyword ranks, GSC and GA4 in one Insights view, with alerts when a keyword moves and a weekly owner report scheduled automatically.

Audits and Content Refresh

Technical and domain SEO audits turn into a fix list; old posts get a refresh plan built from the page's own text and the keywords it should rank for.

Editable Client Reports

Monthly reports are documents the team edits in the browser, then downloads as Word or PDF. Numbers come from the data; AI only writes the commentary.

Ask Neo

A chat drawer on every page (⌘K) that answers questions about a client and can start real jobs: write an article, refresh a page, run a report, add keywords.

Judgement calls

What made it safe to hand to a team.

  • Signed releases. Every runner holds a service key, so runners install only updates signed with a key that never leaves my machine, and refuse downgrades. A forged release was tested and ignored.
  • Parity measured, not claimed. I surveyed the original tool screen by screen, scored a feature matrix and closed gaps until every must-have was covered (about 93% overall parity).
  • Better than the original where it was wrong: article extraction picks the real body instead of the first card on the page, and brand searches count knowledge panels and map packs as ranking.
  • Cheap models for cheap steps. Small labelling steps run on a local model with a Claude fallback; writing always stays on Claude.
